> **TL;DR** — This post walks you through building a pure‑Python GGUF weight loader that lazily memory‑maps tensors on demand. You’ll end up with a runnable module that demonstrates low‑level memory management, binary‑format parsing, and performance‑aware design — ideal for CV signals in ML‑focused engineering roles.

## Why This Project Stands Out on a CV

Hiring managers for ML infrastructure, data‑engineering, and systems‑focused engineering roles see dozens of “hello‑world” notebooks. This project signals three concrete competencies:

1. **Binary‑format literacy** – you can read and parse a non‑trivial binary header (magic number, version, tensor metadata) without a high‑level library.
2. **Memory‑aware programming** – you understand `mmap`, zero‑copy access, and how to keep Python’s garbage collector from eagerly materialising large tensors.
3. **Performance‑first mindset** – lazy initialization, on‑demand loading, and the ability to benchmark load times versus full‑load baselines.

The resulting `gguf_loader` module is importable, has a minimal public API (`load_model(path)` → `Model` object), and can be demonstrated in an interview by loading a 100 MB GGUF model in under 200 ms while keeping RSS footprint low. It also showcases real‑world tools: the GGUF spec (used by llama.cpp), Python’s standard library, and NumPy for tensor handling.
